# Soil Health Card Scheme

**Also known as:** SHC, Soil Health Card, मृदा स्वास्थ्य कार्ड, मृदा आरोग्य पत्रिका, Soil Health & Fertility Management.

**Level:** Central Government of India scheme.
**Ministry:** Ministry of Agriculture and Farmers Welfare, Department of Agriculture & Cooperation.
**Launched:** 19 February 2015.
**Official website:** https://soilhealth.dac.gov.in
**Last verified:** July 2026.

---

## What is the Soil Health Card Scheme

Soil Health Card (SHC) is a Central Government scheme that provides every farmer in India with a card showing the current health status of their farm soil and personalised recommendations for fertilizer and nutrient application. The goal is to reduce over-use of chemical fertilizers, improve soil productivity, and cut input costs.

The scheme is **free of cost** for farmers. Soil samples are collected, analysed at government laboratories, and results are printed on a card that the farmer can carry to make informed decisions.

---

## What Information the Card Contains

Each Soil Health Card shows the following about a farmer's soil:

- **Soil type** (clayey, loamy, sandy, alluvial, etc.)
- **12 soil parameters:**
  - **Major nutrients:** Nitrogen (N), Phosphorus (P), Potassium (K)
  - **Secondary nutrients:** Sulphur (S)
  - **Micronutrients:** Zinc (Zn), Iron (Fe), Copper (Cu), Manganese (Mn), Boron (B)
  - **Physical parameters:** pH (acidity/alkalinity), Electrical Conductivity (EC), Organic Carbon (OC)
- **Fertilizer recommendation** — exact quantity of urea, DAP, MOP, and other fertilizers per acre / hectare for the crops the farmer plans to grow
- **Soil amendment recommendation** — lime, gypsum, or organic manure suggestions based on soil health
- **Crop-specific advice** — best suited crops for the soil type

The recommendations are printed in the **local language** (Hindi, Marathi, Tamil, Telugu, etc.) for easy understanding.

---

## Who is Eligible

**All farmers in India are eligible.** No exclusion criteria. Every farmer holding cultivable land can request a Soil Health Card free of cost.

The scheme aims to cover every farmer in the country. Cards are typically distributed by:

- State Agriculture Department field staff
- Village Level Extension Workers (VLW)
- Krishi Vigyan Kendras (KVK)
- State soil testing laboratories

---

## How to Get a Soil Health Card

Two ways to apply:

**1. Through State Agriculture Department (default)**
- Contact your Taluka Agriculture Officer or Block Development Officer
- Field staff visit villages during pre-cropping seasons to collect soil samples
- No application form needed — you just provide land details and give the sample

**2. Self-request through Krishi Vigyan Kendra (KVK) or CSC**
- Visit your nearest KVK, CSC, or state soil testing lab
- Request soil health testing for your farm
- Provide farm details (survey number, area, current crop)
- Officers guide sample collection and testing

**3. Direct sample submission at soil testing lab**
- Farmers can directly submit soil samples at a State Soil Testing Laboratory
- Cost: FREE for testing under SHC scheme

---

## How Soil Sample is Collected

Proper sample collection is critical for accurate results:

**Where to collect:**
- From 8-10 spots across the field in a zig-zag pattern
- Depth: 0-15 cm for most crops (deeper for orchards / tree crops)
- Avoid: near bunds, tree bases, waterlogged areas, or recently manured spots

**How much:**
- About 500 grams of well-mixed sample from the 8-10 spots
- Use a khurpi, spade, or auger to dig
- Mix all spot samples on a plastic sheet, then take a random 500g subsample

**How to prepare:**
- Air-dry the sample under shade (NOT in direct sun)
- Remove stones, roots, plant matter
- Pack in a clean plastic or cloth bag
- Label with farmer name, village, survey number, date, and current crop

**When to collect:**
- After crop harvest, before next sowing
- Best time: April-May (before kharif) or October-November (before rabi)

---

## Timeline — From Sample to Card

Approximate timeline:

- **Sample collection:** 1 day
- **Lab transport:** 3-7 days
- **Testing:** 15-30 days (varies by lab load)
- **Report generation:** 1-2 weeks
- **Card printing and delivery:** 2-4 weeks

Total: **6-10 weeks** from sample collection to farmer receiving the card. State-level variation exists.

---

## Card Renewal

Soil Health Cards should be renewed **every 3 years**. This is because:

- Nutrient levels change with continuous cropping
- Fertilizer inputs alter soil balance
- pH and organic carbon shift over time

Farmers can request a fresh soil test through the same channels (state agriculture department, KVK, or lab). Renewal is also free.

---

## How to Use the Card

Once you receive your Soil Health Card:

**1. Read the fertilizer recommendation table**
- The card shows how much urea, DAP, MOP, zinc, sulphur etc. to apply per acre or hectare for specific crops
- Recommendations are usually given for 3-4 popular crops in your district
- Follow the recommended amount — do NOT apply more (over-application harms soil and reduces yield)

**2. Apply soil amendments if suggested**
- If pH is too low (acidic) → apply lime
- If pH is too high (alkaline) → apply gypsum
- If organic carbon is low → apply farmyard manure, compost, or green manure

**3. Adjust cropping plan**
- If soil is deficient in a particular nutrient, prefer crops that need less of it
- If soil is very fertile in some nutrients, plan crop rotations to use those

**4. Take to your fertilizer retailer**
- Show the card to your local dealer or Krishi Kendra
- They can help you pick the right combination of fertilizers based on the recommendation

---

## Benefits Reported by Farmers

Farmers who followed SHC recommendations reported:

- **10-25% reduction** in fertilizer costs (less over-application)
- **8-15% increase** in crop yield in some cases
- **Better soil condition** over time
- Fewer nutrient deficiency symptoms in crops

The scheme has been credited with reducing chemical fertilizer overuse in states where implementation was strong.

---

## Common Issues

Common problems farmers face with SHC:

- **Delay in getting the card** — labs are often overloaded, especially before sowing seasons
- **Card lost or damaged** — request a duplicate through the state portal or agriculture officer
- **Card in unfamiliar language** — cards should be printed in the local language; if not, contact the district agriculture office
- **Recommendations for crops not grown** — farmers can request updated recommendations by informing the officer of actual planned crops
- **No follow-up support** — some farmers do not receive extension guidance on how to interpret the card. Contact KVK or Kisan Call Center (1800-180-1551) for help
